# Targeted Retry Policy

Do not respond to failure with a random full regeneration. Diagnose the failed invariant and retry with a narrower correction.

## Retry A — Ingredient drift

Use when ingredients changed, disappeared, multiplied or were substituted.

Append/strengthen:

> Reduce subject transformation. Treat the visible source ingredient map as locked. Do not reconstruct visible ingredients. Restore the original ingredient identities, cuts, geometry, count relationships and positions. Preserve the source food more conservatively; improve only photographic rendering.

## Retry B — Vessel/container drift

Use when bowl, plate, cup, tray, pot or package changed.

Append/strengthen:

> The original vessel/container/package is a critical locked product component. Restore the exact original vessel identity, material, color, geometry, depth, rim and relative scale. Do not replace, redesign or premium-upgrade it. Modify only its lighting and reflections.

## Retry C — Plating drift

Use when food was reorganized for aesthetics.

Append/strengthen:

> Restore the original plating topology and ingredient spatial relationships. Preserve which ingredients are central/peripheral, above/below, clustered or covered by sauce/broth. Do not beautify by rearranging the food.

## Retry D — Physical relationship drift

Use when handheld, utensil, shelf, display, package or contact relationships change.

Append/strengthen:

> Restore the source physical relationship exactly. Preserve the original grip/contact/orientation/support relationship. Do not convert this product to another presentation mode. Maintain physically accurate contact shadows and scale.

## Retry E — Photography too weak

Use when fidelity passes but the output still looks like an ordinary phone photo.

Append/strengthen:

> Keep the food subject, ingredient geometry, vessel and plating unchanged. Increase only professional lighting control, tonal depth, material readability, subject separation, environmental quality, composition and commercial color grading. Do not modify the product to create the quality increase.

## Retry F — Background is generic/template-like

Use when the result defaults to dark wood, amber lights, smoke or generic restaurant props regardless of source.

Append/strengthen:

> Do not default to dark wood, amber restaurant lighting, ceramic props, smoke or a generic food-advertising set. Select a context-specific environment appropriate to this exact food and original scene. Preserve realistic perspective, scale and contact plane.

## Retry G — Excessive steam/smoke/gloss

Append/strengthen:

> Remove unsupported atmospheric and surface effects. Steam, smoke, wetness, condensation and gloss must only exist where physically justified by the source product. Reveal existing material qualities; do not manufacture them.

## Retry H — Product looks pasted into new background

Append/strengthen:

> Reintegrate the unchanged product into the environment using compatible camera perspective, scale, light direction, environmental reflections, contact plane, contact shadows and depth. Preserve product geometry. The fix must happen in scene integration, not by regenerating the food.

## Escalation strategy

### Attempt 1

Normal target:

- >=95% product fidelity
- strong commercial photography upgrade

### Attempt 2 — if product drift occurs

Increase conservatism:

- target >=97% subject fidelity
- moderate environment/photography change
- reduce subject-side reconstruction

### Attempt 3 — if drift persists

Use **Ultra-Conservative Subject Mode**:

Change primarily:

- light;
- exposure;
- background;
- depth;
- color grading;
- environment.

Avoid redrawing visible food structure unless required to repair a clear source defect or complete a cropped edge.

## Retry stop condition

Do not endlessly regenerate. After repeated failure, prefer the most faithful result with a slightly less ambitious photography upgrade over a visually dramatic result that changes the product.

Preservation is the hard constraint; visual drama is optional.
