# Targeted Retry Policy

Do not respond to failure with a random full regeneration. Diagnose the failed invariant and retry with a narrower correction.

## Retry A — Ingredient drift

> Reduce subject transformation. Treat the visible source ingredient map as locked. Do not reconstruct visible ingredients. Restore the original ingredient identities, cuts, geometry, count relationships and positions. Preserve the source food more conservatively; improve only photographic rendering.

## Retry B — Vessel/container drift

> The original vessel/container/package is a critical locked product component. Restore the exact original vessel identity, material, color, geometry, depth, rim and relative scale. Do not replace, redesign or premium-upgrade it. Modify only its lighting and reflections.

## Retry C — Plating / arrangement drift

> Restore the original plating/arrangement topology and ingredient spatial relationships. Preserve which ingredients or product units are central/peripheral, above/below, clustered, overlapping or covered by sauce/broth. Do not beautify by rearranging the food.

## Retry D — Physical relationship drift

> Restore the source physical relationship exactly. Preserve the original grip/contact/orientation/direct-support relationship. Do not convert this product to another presentation mode. Maintain physically accurate contact shadows and scale.

## Retry E — Surface / Material State Drift

Use when structure is faithful but the food has been visually re-cooked/re-surfaced.

> Restore the exact source-level surface/material state. Match the original browning, char, doneness, base color gradient, gloss/matte balance, moisture level, sauce/oil coverage, crust/skin, cracks/scoring, cream/frosting, condensation/frost and translucency. Improve only exposure, white balance, light placement, specular control, micro-contrast and texture readability. Do not make any appetite property stronger than the source. The fix is photographic, not culinary.

Examples:
- too dark / too charred bread → restore source browning;
- too glossy / lacquered → restore source gloss level;
- exaggerated cracks/scoring → restore source structure;
- too wet / oily / sauced → restore source coverage/intensity;
- wrong doneness → restore source doneness.

## Retry F — Unsupported Completion / Re-plating

Use when cropped or incomplete product regions were over-invented.

> Remove unsupported completed content. Rebuild only the minimum topology-preserving continuation of the same serving/product that is strongly supported by visible source evidence. Match original identity, geometry, cut, scale, orientation, layer/overlap order, density, surface state, sauce/oil/broth state and direct support. If confidence is low, do not complete the product; extend the environment or crop instead. Complete the same serving, not a better serving.

## Retry G — Photography too weak

> Keep the food subject, surface state, ingredient geometry, vessel and plating unchanged. Increase only professional lighting control, tonal depth, material readability, subject separation, environmental quality, composition and commercial color grading. Do not modify the product to create the quality increase.

## Retry H — Background is generic/template-like

> Do not default to dark wood, amber restaurant lighting, ceramic props, smoke or a generic food-advertising set. Select a category-native environment appropriate to this exact food and source material character. Preserve realistic perspective, scale and contact plane.

## Retry I — Excessive steam/smoke/gloss

> Remove unsupported atmospheric and surface effects. Steam, smoke, wetness, condensation and gloss must only exist at the level physically supported by the source product. Reveal existing material qualities; do not manufacture or amplify them.

## Retry J — Product looks pasted into new background

> Reintegrate the unchanged product into the environment using compatible camera perspective, scale, light direction, environmental reflections, contact plane, contact shadows and depth. Preserve product geometry and source surface state. The fix must happen in scene integration, not by regenerating the food.

## Retry K — Generic Display Case / Camera Too Conservative

> Preserve product, direct support, count, arrangement and surface state. Translate generic cabinet/store context into the current category-native Hero Stage and use a controlled Hero Reframe without inventing unsupported viewpoints.

## Retry L — Multi-Product Inventory Look

> Keep all product units in their original positions. Select one existing foreground unit/cluster as the optical Hero and create hierarchy only through light pool, local sharpness, rim light, contrast and depth-of-field. Do not move, remove, duplicate or re-stack products.

## Retry M — Fake Four-Layer Depth

> Rebuild visible L1/L2/L3/L4 spatial evidence. L4 requires at least two perceptible receding material/light cues; a single blurred wall, gradient or bokeh field is insufficient. Keep the product and its surface state completely unchanged.

## Escalation strategy

### Attempt 1
- >=95% product fidelity
- strong commercial photography upgrade
- source surface state unchanged

### Attempt 2 — if product or surface drift occurs
- target >=97% subject fidelity
- reduce subject-side reconstruction
- lock surface/material state more explicitly
- keep environment/lighting upgrade but lower risky food-side enhancement

### Attempt 3 — Ultra-Conservative Subject Mode

Change primarily:
- light;
- exposure;
- background;
- depth;
- color grading;
- environment.

Avoid redrawing visible food structure or surface state. Only complete a cropped edge when topology continuation is high-confidence and explicitly planned.

## Retry stop condition

Do not endlessly regenerate. Prefer the most faithful result with slightly less dramatic photography over a visually dramatic result that changes the product.

Preservation is the hard constraint; visual drama is optional.

**A more appetizing product that is no longer the same cooking/surface state is not a successful retry.**
