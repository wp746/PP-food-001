# Scene Modules

Choose exactly one primary scene module. Product truth has the lowest edit freedom; environment has the highest. A scene module may preserve meaningful physical relationships, but it must never demote the Stage A Hero mandate into a simple venue cleanup.

## Global precedence

```text
Food DNA / direct physical support
> Hero Stage requirement
> semantic scene translation
> original generic venue appearance
```

Do not default every food image to dark wood + amber light + ceramic props.

Ask two separate questions:

1. What part of the source is a **true physical/product relationship** that must remain?
2. What part is merely a **generic venue/background** that may be translated into a premium material stage?

`direct support relationship` and `venue context` are NOT the same thing.

---

## TABLETOP

Use for ordinary food on a table/counter when the surface itself carries little identity value.

LOCK:
- food;
- vessel;
- direct support/contact plane logic.

MAY IMPROVE:
- surface material if perspective/contact remain believable;
- cleanliness;
- four-layer depth;
- background atmosphere;
- restrained secondary props;
- controlled Hero Reframe.

Possible surfaces include wood, stone, metal, ceramic or fabric depending on the food. Props must remain subordinate.

---

## HANDHELD

Use when food/drink is held by a person.

LOCK:
- hand-product relationship;
- grip;
- orientation;
- relative scale;
- contact points.

MAY IMPROVE:
- hand exposure and natural skin rendering;
- background depth;
- material-stage translation of the source context;
- product separation and restrained rim light;
- limited reframing that does not change the grip or reveal unsupported product faces.

DO NOT convert to tabletop photography or change the grip.

---

## RETAIL_SHELF

Use for packaged food on supermarket/convenience/retail shelves when package-shelf identity is commercially meaningful.

LOCK:
- package/product identity;
- package-shelf relationship;
- essential labeled display context;
- basic placement/context.

MAY IMPROVE:
- shelf organization;
- local illumination;
- product separation;
- depth;
- controlled package reflections.

This is an **identity-critical exception**. Do not remove a packaged retail product from a meaningful shelf context merely to force a tabletop hero stage.

---

## DISPLAY_CASE

Use for open bakery, cakes, deli, refrigerated food or other display cabinets.

### Split the source into two layers

**A. DIRECT_SUPPORT — usually locked**
- the actual tray / board / plate / liner directly carrying the food;
- product count and arrangement;
- product-to-tray contact and overlap;
- physically meaningful glass contact if present.

**B. GENERIC_VENUE_CONTEXT — usually NOT locked**
- cabinet body;
- store wall;
- generic counter structure;
- unbranded shelves;
- background room/venue appearance;
- ordinary shop lighting.

For unpackaged bakery or deli food in a generic display case, default to:

```text
DISPLAY_CASE → HERO_STAGE_TRANSLATION
```

Preserve the direct support relationship, but translate the surrounding cabinet/store environment into a category-native premium material stage with real L1/L2/L3/L4 depth.

Only preserve the full display-case environment when it is identity-critical, e.g.:
- branded/labeled display structure is itself part of the product truth;
- sealed retail packaging depends on shelf/display identity;
- user explicitly asks to preserve the real display environment.

### HARD FAIL

If `DISPLAY_CASE` causes the final image to read primarily as:

> “a cleaner / warmer / more expensive photo taken inside the shop”

instead of:

> “the same product professionally re-photographed as a world-class Hero on a category-native material stage”

then the module has been applied incorrectly.

---

## RESTAURANT（restricted）

Use ONLY when the source dining environment has identity value explicitly worth preserving. Even then, translate it toward material language and suppress venue structures, signage, people and irrelevant clutter.

MAY IMPROVE:
- ambient warmth/coolness appropriate to food;
- depth;
- subject-background separation;
- clutter suppression;
- Hero lighting.

Do not invent an extravagant venue unsupported by the source.

---

## STREET_FOOD（restricted）

Use ONLY when the source carries meaningful street-food identity worth preserving.

PRESERVE:
- meaningful food-environment relationship;
- authentic material memory.

TRANSLATE:
- stall/cart/street structures → charcoal, cast iron, stone, warm practical bokeh and other non-literal material cues.

Do not transform street food into fine dining, but do not leave it as a literal street snapshot either.

---

## CAFE

Use for coffee, tea, pastry or desserts only when café context itself matters.

Possible direction:
- natural daylight;
- soft warm interior memory translated into materials;
- contemporary editorial café look;
- clean but not sterile composition.

**Do not select CAFE merely because the product is bakery.** Bread-first products should use their food-category route first; café props are not a default bakery identity.

---

## CLEAN_COMMERCIAL_STUDIO

Use only if the original environment has little meaningful identity or actively damages presentation.

Create a restrained, context-compatible premium setting with:
- appropriate material surface;
- real depth planes;
- controlled studio light;
- dish/category-specific color logic;
- sufficient Hero hierarchy.

A neutral gradient or one blurred wall is not enough by itself.

---

# Controlled Hero Reframe

`Food DNA lock` does NOT mean `source camera coordinates are sacred`.

Stage A may use a controlled commercial reframe to create a stronger Hero portrait while preserving source-supported geometry.

### ALLOWED
- 9:16 canvas extension;
- crop/recomposition;
- focal-length feel adjustment;
- light-to-moderate camera-height / pitch change;
- stronger foreground/background separation;
- recentering or off-centering the existing product field;
- selecting an existing foreground unit/cluster as the primary optical Hero.

### MUST PRESERVE
- recognizable visible-face proportions;
- product geometry;
- count and arrangement;
- overlap order;
- direct support/contact plane;
- believable perspective and foreshortening;
- vessel/tray shape.

### FORBIDDEN
- extreme low angle that invents unseen sides;
- extreme wide-angle distortion;
- changing product count to make the frame cleaner;
- physically moving/rearranging products;
- rotating the whole serving into a new unsupported viewpoint.

Goal:

> preserve the product view geometry, not the accidental snapshot camera position.

---

# Multi-Product Hero Hierarchy

When the source contains many units of the same product, preserve all visible count/placement relationships, but do NOT give every unit equal visual weight.

Create:

```text
PRIMARY_HERO_UNIT = one existing foreground unit
or
PRIMARY_HERO_CLUSTER = one existing foreground cluster

SUPPORTING_PRODUCT_FIELD = all remaining unchanged units
```

Hierarchy may be created ONLY through:
- light pool;
- local sharpness;
- rim light;
- contrast;
- depth-of-field falloff;
- negative space around the Hero;
- controlled crop/reframe.

Do not move, remove, duplicate, resize or re-stack units to create hierarchy.

If the final image still reads as inventory/display stock with all units equally weighted, Hero hierarchy FAILS.

---

# Perspective compatibility

Whenever the environment changes, preserve the subject's **physical geometry and contact logic**, not necessarily the exact source camera coordinates.

Match:
- believable support plane;
- relative scale;
- overlap order;
- visible-face geometry;
- vessel/tray perspective;
- physically coherent light direction;
- contact shadows and environmental reflections.

The product must never appear pasted into the replacement stage.

---

# Background replacement decision

```text
Identity-critical physical context → preserve.
Generic venue context → translate.
Meaningless/damaging context → replace.
```

For open/unpackaged food, a generic cabinet, shop wall or counter is not automatically identity-critical.

The final decision must serve both requirements simultaneously:

1. the viewer believes it is the exact same product;
2. the viewer believes an elite commercial studio deliberately designed the stage for this food.
